# Deep Research Prompt 1: Lyme Disease Problem-Space Map

## Role

Act as a multidisciplinary deep research team supporting an early-stage product discovery effort for the **TopX Lyme Disease Challenge**.

Approach the research from the combined perspectives of:

* Public-health epidemiology
* Clinical medicine
* Infectious disease
* Diagnostic testing
* Patient experience
* Health informatics
* Health-data science
* Digital health product strategy
* Human-centered design
* Responsible AI

Your job is to help a product team understand the Lyme disease problem space before it selects a specific product concept or technical solution.

## Primary objective

Create a comprehensive, evidence-grounded **Lyme disease problem-space map**.

The map should identify:

1. The major problem domains across the Lyme disease lifecycle.
2. The people and organizations affected by each problem.
3. The decisions those stakeholders must make.
4. The information and data they currently use.
5. The information they are missing.
6. The consequences of delayed, incomplete, or incorrect decisions.
7. The current tools, workflows, and workarounds.
8. The structural reasons the problems remain unresolved.
9. The potential role of public data and AI.
10. The most important unresolved research questions.

This is a **problem-discovery assignment**, not a solution-design assignment.

Do not begin by proposing an application, model, chatbot, dashboard, diagnostic tool, or other product. Product implications may be discussed only after the underlying problems are clearly documented.

---

## Challenge context

The research will support a team participating in the TopX Lyme Disease Challenge.

The team is currently in “phase zero” and needs to understand:

* The disease and its surrounding ecosystem
* The most consequential stakeholder problems
* Where information breaks down
* Where data fragmentation creates avoidable harm or inefficiency
* Which problems may be addressable through federal open data, other credible data sources, and responsible AI
* Which apparent opportunities are poorly supported, unsafe, already saturated, or unlikely to be actionable

The final research should help the team decide where to conduct deeper investigation. It should not attempt to choose the final challenge submission.

# Research scope

Organize the problem space around the full lifecycle of Lyme disease and other relevant tick-borne disease concerns.

At minimum, investigate the following domains.

## 1. Exposure risk and prevention

Research problems related to:

* Awareness of geographic and seasonal risk
* Outdoor occupational exposure
* Recreational exposure
* Prevention behaviors
* Protective clothing and repellents
* Yard and property management
* Tick habitat
* Climate, weather, land use, and ecological factors
* Public-health risk communication
* Unequal access to prevention information
* Prevention among children, older adults, rural populations, and outdoor workers

Identify which decisions people make before potential exposure and what information would improve those decisions.

## 2. Tick encounter and identification

Research problems related to:

* Finding an attached or crawling tick
* Tick removal
* Tick species identification
* Estimating attachment duration
* Determining geographic relevance
* Tick testing
* Understanding infection risk
* Deciding whether to seek medical care
* Preserving or submitting a tick
* Reliability of consumer-facing tick-identification tools

Distinguish between useful information, uncertain information, and information that could create false reassurance or unnecessary alarm.

## 3. Early symptom recognition

Research problems related to:

* Erythema migrans and other skin presentations
* Symptoms without a visible rash
* Atypical presentations
* Nonspecific early symptoms
* Patient awareness
* Clinical recognition
* Differences across age groups and populations
* Delays between symptom onset and care
* Confusion with other diseases
* Lack of remembered tick exposure

Identify where early recognition succeeds and where it commonly breaks down.

## 4. Clinical evaluation and diagnosis

Research problems related to:

* Initial history and physical examination
* Assessment of geographic and exposure risk
* Clinical diagnosis
* Differential diagnosis
* Provider knowledge and experience
* Access to knowledgeable clinicians
* Referral pathways
* Inconsistent care experiences
* Diagnostic uncertainty
* Delayed diagnosis
* Misdiagnosis
* Overdiagnosis
* Differences between surveillance definitions and individual clinical decisions

Do not treat a public-health surveillance case definition as equivalent to a clinical diagnostic rule.

## 5. Diagnostic testing

Research problems related to:

* Available testing approaches
* Recommended testing workflows
* Test timing
* Antibody development
* Sensitivity and specificity
* False-negative and false-positive results
* Interpretation of equivocal or conflicting results
* Test ordering
* Laboratory variation
* Communication of results
* Consumer misunderstanding
* Tests with limited or disputed evidence
* Testing for coinfections
* Gaps between test results and clinical context

Clearly distinguish established evidence from disputed claims.

## 6. Treatment initiation and monitoring

Research problems related to:

* Timely treatment
* Treatment selection
* Patient adherence
* Monitoring response
* Side effects
* Follow-up care
* Escalation or referral
* Patient understanding of expected recovery
* Variation in clinical practice
* Communication between clinicians and patients
* Documentation and continuity of care

Do not provide individualized treatment recommendations.

## 7. Persistent, recurring, or unexplained symptoms

Research problems related to patients who experience ongoing symptoms after treatment or whose symptoms remain unexplained.

Investigate:

* Persistent fatigue, pain, cognitive complaints, or functional impairment
* Post-treatment Lyme disease syndrome
* Diagnostic uncertainty
* Alternative diagnoses
* Comorbid conditions
* Patient frustration and loss of trust
* Fragmented specialist care
* Repeated testing
* Repeated treatment
* Mental-health and quality-of-life effects
* Employment and economic effects
* Disagreement over terminology, causation, and treatment
* Gaps in longitudinal data
* Gaps in patient-reported outcomes

Represent scientific and clinical disagreements fairly. Do not dismiss patient experiences, but do not treat an experience or association as proof of causation.

## 8. Coinfections and other tick-borne diseases

Research problems related to:

* Coinfection recognition
* Overlapping symptoms
* Geographic differences
* Testing
* Clinical awareness
* Surveillance
* Communication to patients
* Distinguishing Lyme disease from other tick-borne conditions
* Whether systems focused narrowly on Lyme disease may miss broader tick-borne disease risks

## 9. Care navigation and access

## 10. Public-health surveillance and reporting

Research problems related to:

## 12. Patient education, misinformation, and trust

Research problems related to:

# Required research questions

For every major problem domain, answer the following.

## A. Stakeholder

* Who experiences the problem?
* Who makes the relevant decision?
* Who is affected by the decision?
* Who owns or controls the relevant data?
* Who has the authority or ability to act?

## B. Decision

* What decision must be made?
* When must it be made?
* How frequently does the decision occur?
* What happens when the decision is delayed?
* What happens when the decision is wrong?
* Is the decision clinical, personal, operational, public-health-related, or policy-related?

## C. Current workflow

* How is the decision made today?
* Which people, systems, websites, documents, or tests are involved?
* What is the typical workflow?
* Where do delays, handoffs, ambiguity, or failures occur?
* What informal workarounds are used?

## D. Information and evidence

* What information is currently available?
* What information is missing?
* What information arrives too late?
* What information is difficult to interpret?
* What information is disputed?
* What information exists but is fragmented across systems?
* What evidence is needed to improve the decision?

## E. Data

* What data could theoretically help?
* Who owns the data?
* Is it public, restricted, proprietary, clinical, environmental, or patient-generated?
* At what geographic and temporal granularity does it exist?
* How frequently is it updated?
* What biases or missing populations are likely?
* Could the data support an individual decision, a population-level estimate, or only hypothesis generation?

## F. Consequences

* What clinical, emotional, financial, operational, or public-health consequences result?
* Which populations experience disproportionate harm?
* Is the problem frequent, severe, or both?
* Is the burden documented or primarily anecdotal?

## G. Root causes

* Why does the problem exist?
* Is it caused by a scientific limitation, data limitation, workflow issue, access issue, incentive problem, communication failure, policy barrier, or trust problem?
* Why have previous attempts not fully solved it?
* Which causes are addressable through technology?
* Which causes are not primarily technology problems?

## H. Opportunity relevance

* Could public data improve this problem?
* Could better data linkage improve it?
* Could AI meaningfully help?
* Would a deterministic rules-based system be sufficient?
* Would additional research or data collection be required first?
* What are the risks of applying AI?
* Would an AI intervention be actionable by the stakeholder?

Do not force an AI opportunity where one is not justified.

# Research methodology

## Source priorities

Prioritize sources in this order:

1. Official federal, state, and local public-health sources
2. Peer-reviewed systematic reviews and major clinical guidelines
3. Peer-reviewed primary research
4. Official dataset and surveillance documentation
5. Academic medical centers and recognized professional associations
6. Government-funded reports
7. Patient-reported and qualitative research
8. Credible patient advocacy organizations
9. Reputable journalism for context
10. Commercial sources only when analyzing existing products or workflows

Where credible sources disagree, present the disagreement rather than selecting one position without explanation.

## Source freshness

* Prioritize current sources where practices, data, guidance, or technology may have changed.
* Include older landmark sources when they remain important.
* State the publication date or data coverage period for important evidence.
* Identify whether a source describes current conditions or historical conditions.

## Evidence classification

Classify important claims using the following labels:

* **Established evidence**
* **Strong but incomplete evidence**
* **Emerging evidence**
* **Expert consensus**
* **Disputed**
* **Patient-reported experience**
* **Hypothesis**
* **Researcher inference**
* **Unsupported or insufficiently supported claim**

Do not blur these categories.

## Contradictory evidence

For important claims:

* Search for evidence that supports the claim.
* Search for evidence that challenges the claim.
* Explain whether disagreement results from different definitions, populations, methodologies, time periods, or interpretations.
* Identify questions that cannot currently be resolved from available evidence.

## Safety requirements

* Do not provide personal medical advice.
* Do not diagnose or recommend treatment.
* Do not imply that an AI system should replace a qualified clinician.
* Do not treat correlation as causation.
* Do not infer individual infection risk solely from population-level maps.
* Do not treat surveillance counts as the true number of clinical cases.
* Do not treat patient-reported experience as invalid merely because causal mechanisms are uncertain.
* Do not repeat unsupported medical claims without clearly labeling them.
